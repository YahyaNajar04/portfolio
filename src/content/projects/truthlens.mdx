Paste a news URL, get a credibility verdict with reasoning. Three of us built it
over a weekend at the Great Malaysia AI Hackathon. I did the model training —
roughly twenty runs across twenty-eight hours to get a classifier that converged.

## The architecture was the good decision

Most teams that weekend threw an LLM at the whole problem and got something
unmeasurable. We split it:

- **TF-IDF + LightGBM** does the classification — word 1–2-grams and character
  3–5-grams unioned into one feature space, gradient-boosted into three classes
  (SUPPORTS / REFUTES / NEI), with balanced class weights.
- **Llama 3.1 on Bedrock** writes the human-readable explanation — the part an
  LLM is actually good at.
- Retrieval against Wikipedia supplies evidence for the verdict.

A small discriminative model making the decision means the decision is cheap,
fast, and — crucially — *measurable*. The LLM never touches the label.

## Two flaws I found a year later

I reopened the repo in 2026 to write this page, and found things I hadn't seen
at the time.

**The model never saw any evidence.** The training script pairs each FEVER claim
with a canned hypothesis from a fixed list — "This claim is true.", "Evidence
contradicts this claim." — concatenates them, and vectorises. No Wikipedia
passage ever reaches the classifier. So it isn't verifying claims against
evidence; it's guessing whether a sentence *sounds* true from its wording. The
retrieval layer existed in the product and not in the model.

**The split leaked.** Every claim expands into ~14 template pairs *before*
`train_test_split` runs, so the same claim lands in both train and validation.
The 0.673 is measuring partly memorisation. Split by claim ID and it drops.

## What I'd build instead

Retrieve evidence with FAISS over a Wikipedia index, feed claim-plus-evidence to
a cross-encoder so the model actually reads what it's judging, split by claim ID
before any augmentation, and report on FEVER's real test set rather than a
self-made validation slice.

The number would almost certainly be lower. It would also mean something.
